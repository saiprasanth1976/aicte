Create a 60-second cinematic, ultra-realistic promotional teaser video for a software developer tool that visualizes AI coding-assistant credit/token consumption. The genre is high-budget tech thriller mixed with dark workplace comedy and absurd internet humour. Make it feel like a professional movie trailer, not a generic corporate advertisement or a slideshow of AI images.

VIDEO STYLE AND CONTINUITY

- Cinematic 16:9 widescreen, realistic human characters, premium lighting, detailed environments, dramatic camera movements, natural expressions, polished colour grading, and immersive sound design.
- Use smooth match cuts, whip pans, continuous camera movement, motion blur, and seamless transitions. Avoid abrupt jump cuts, random scene changes, frozen-looking characters, distorted faces, extra fingers, and inconsistent environments.
- Build tension gradually, then escalate into ridiculous comedy, before ending with a sleek, confident product reveal.
- Maintain a consistent visual identity: dark navy, electric blue, subtle red warning lights, and clean modern software aesthetics.
- Use realistic software-development environments, modern offices, multiple monitors, and authentic developer behaviour.
- Keep the pacing energetic, with something visually interesting happening every few seconds.
- Important: Render character dialogue, headlines, percentages, product names, and interface text as editable overlays during post-production if the generator cannot reproduce text accurately. Do not invent readable software interfaces or fake product capabilities.

SCENE 1 — THE INNOCENT COMMAND (0–7 SECONDS)
Open with an extreme close-up of a developer's fingers typing a command into a code editor in a dark, modern office. The terminal cursor blinks. He confidently clicks “Allow All Commands in Session,” leans back, smiles, and relaxes as his AI coding assistant begins working. He closes his eyes for a quick nap.

The camera slowly pushes towards the monitor. A usage indicator begins climbing rapidly: 12%… 38%… 67%… 96%.

The music becomes increasingly tense. Warning lights subtly illuminate his face. He suddenly wakes up, looks at the screen, freezes in disbelief, and screams:

“WHAT THE TOKENS?!”

The scream echoes dramatically through the office. Hit the sound with a cinematic bass impact.

SCENE 2 — THE GLOBAL TOKEN CRISIS (7–14 SECONDS)
Use the echo of the scream as a seamless transition into an antique world map. Electric-blue pulses spread across continents, connecting developers in different countries.

Move rapidly through a connected montage: developers in Bengaluru, London, New York, Tokyo, and Berlin staring at their screens in disbelief. One grabs his head. Another slowly removes his glasses. Another checks the usage indicator three times, hoping it is a mistake.

Their reactions synchronise into a worldwide chorus:

“WHAT THE TOKENS?!”

Layer the voices with dramatic echoes, fast camera movements, and rising orchestral tension. Make it funny through the seriousness of the presentation.

SCENE 3 — BREAKING NEWS (14–24 SECONDS)
Transition smoothly into an overdramatic international breaking-news broadcast. A serious news anchor sits in a sleek studio surrounded by red warning graphics and animated data visualisations.

The anchor announces with absolute seriousness:

“BREAKING NEWS: A DEVELOPER HAS EXHAUSTED HIS MONTHLY AI CREDITS. SOURCES CONFIRM HE IS NOW EXPLORING THE CONCEPT OF PLANNED LEAVE.”

Show a scrolling news ticker with the headline:

“DEVELOPING: JACOB HAS CONSUMED 95% OF HIS MONTHLY QUOTA IN JUST 10 DAYS.”

Cut to a developer sitting miserably at his desk, counting imaginary tokens on his fingers.

The anchor continues:

“PRODUCTIVITY REMAINS UNDER INVESTIGATION. THE NEXT QUOTA RESET IS STILL WEEKS AWAY.”

Add dramatic news stingers, a subtle camera push towards the anchor, and a tiny pause before the final punchline.

SCENE 4 — LET THE AI COOK (24–34 SECONDS)
Return to a modern developer's office. A confident developer clicks “Allow All Commands in Session,” grins at his screen, and says:

“Let the AI cook.”

He walks away to get popcorn while the AI coding assistant works in the background.

Show a smooth, continuous sequence of the popcorn being prepared, the developer returning, and the monitor coming into view.

The usage indicator now reads 96%.

He stops dead. His eyes widen. The popcorn bowl slips from his hands. Popcorn flies through the air in spectacular slow motion as the soundtrack drops to silence.

He whispers:

“I WAS GONE FOR TWO MINUTES.”

Hold on his horrified expression for a brief comedic beat, then hit the scene with a bass drop.

SCENE 5 — THE DEVELOPER BRAIN FREEZE (34–40 SECONDS)
A different developer discovers that his AI credits are disappearing faster than expected. He stares at the monitor, his eyes widening comically.

In a brief, surreal, non-graphic cartoon-style visual effect, a miniature cartoon brain pops out above his head, bounces once, and drops back into place. He immediately shuts his laptop with exaggerated caution, locks it away, and looks around suspiciously.

He says quietly:

“Nobody touch my tokens.”

Make the moment absurd, quick, and visually memorable. Use playful sound effects without gore or disturbing imagery.

SCENE 6 — THE QUESTIONS (40–47 SECONDS)
Build a rapid, seamless montage of developers looking directly into the camera or staring at their screens. Each asks one question, with the words appearing as bold, cinematic overlays:

“HOW MUCH DID I USE?”

“WHERE DID IT GO?”

“WHY IS IT BURNING SO FAST?”

“WHO SPENT ALL MY CREDITS?”

The final developer slowly turns towards the camera and asks:

“AND WHY DID NOBODY WARN ME?”

At this moment, abruptly cut the chaotic music to silence. The screen goes dark for a fraction of a second.

SCENE 7 — THE PRODUCT REVEAL (47–60 SECONDS)
A clean electric-blue light sweeps across the dark screen. The music transforms from chaotic thriller music into confident, futuristic technology music.

Reveal a polished, modern developer-tool interface integrated into a realistic coding workflow. Show a dashboard with clear, elegant visualisations of AI usage, supported activity or session breakdowns, usage trends, and quota warnings. Use only features the actual product supports; use genuine screen recordings or screenshots for the real interface wherever possible.

Show a developer calmly reviewing usage insights, identifying a high-consumption session, and receiving a warning that their monthly quota may run out early.

The atmosphere changes from panic to control. The developer smiles, understands where the credits went, and gets back to work confidently.

Display the final message in large, clean typography:

“STOP GUESSING WHERE YOUR CREDITS WENT.”

Then:

“START UNDERSTANDING WHERE THEY GO.”

Finish with the actual product name and logo, followed by the tagline:

“YOUR AI USAGE. YOUR VISIBILITY. YOUR CONTROL.”

End on a crisp, premium title card with a subtle electric-blue glow and a powerful cinematic audio finish.

OVERALL CREATIVE DIRECTION
The first 47 seconds should feel like a global emergency caused by out-of-control AI coding credits, presented with the seriousness of a major world crisis and the absurdity of a developer meme. The final 13 seconds should reveal a genuinely useful solution and make the audience feel relief.

The humour must come from relatable developer experiences, dramatic overreactions, impeccable timing, and escalating absurdity—not random jokes.

Prioritise visual storytelling, fluid transitions, expressive performances, cinematic sound design, and strong pacing. The finished video should look like a premium teaser for an innovative developer-tool launch at a major technology conference.
